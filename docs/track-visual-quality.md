# Track materials and continuous environments

Updated 5 October 2026. The shared renderer serves all 38 original arcade routes. Driving layouts, elevations, collision widths, ramp physics, progression and vehicle assets are unchanged. The scenery remains authored browser-game geometry; this is not a claim of Asphalt or console-game parity.

The Norway relief update and refreshed preview have passed renderer and responsive gameplay review. Source commit `a556613` is pushed and deployed after the normal complete test/build gate passed. Exact live-byte verification and the production lobby/start/pause smoke check passed. See the [5 October release report](../reports/crowd-fjord-release-2026-10-05.md).

## Visible work

- Roads retain the real asphalt colour/normal/roughness set at its documented three-metre scale. Sector wear preserves the underlying roughness map.
- Sidewalks, terrace decks, access paths and urban forecourts use actual stone paving at 2.12 m scale. The previous stained wall photograph is no longer used on those floors. Terrace masonry shares the paving batch, retaining its draw bound.
- Tapered crash barriers retain their original collision envelope. Their concrete texture now contributes 24% of surface colour and a restrained normal amplitude, after renderer review found conspicuous brown wall stains. Broad urban ground uses 15% asphalt variation over a muted base.
- City plots have paved forecourts, raised borders, soil/hedge strips and checked paths to sidewalks and neighbouring plots. Coastal plots retain submerged foundations. These are actual meshes, capped at 30 mobile / 46 desktop plots, with three material draws.
- Continuous verges bridge the outer sidewalks to surrounding land; coastal verges have submerged skirts. Full strip footprints are checked against the entire route.
- San Francisco has a continuous, gently graded shoreline with an open-water suspension crossing. Tree roots and house foundations sample the actual rendered terrain triangles. The former isolated discs, steep bank strips and detached random towers are removed. Seventeen bay homes have projecting front bays, side/rear windows, sills, mullions and capped chimneys. Houses and vegetation are omitted through the bridge span.
- Fuji and other inland parkland routes have continuous rolling ground with flat pads blended around roads and fixed venue structures, replacing isolated round relief mounds. Road-adjacent grid cells are capped below the full driving surface.
- Nearby terrain retains the 90-metre aerial photograph and adds two-metre CC0 Sparse Grass colour grain and OpenGL normal detail. Grain fades between 45 and 140 metres, without additional draw calls or geometry displacement. Joined outer terrain rings and a textured eroded mountain replace Fuji’s smooth cone and abrupt rectangular ground edge.
- Norway's ten isolated cone mountains and separate snow caps are replaced by two continuous glacial ranges with broad shoulders, branching gullies and irregular ridgelines. Meadow, exposed stone and snow share each range's surface. Dedicated CC0 cliff colour and OpenGL normal maps cover the ranges. The relief update below replaces vertical Z/Y projection with cross-slope mapping. The ranges stay outside the road and recovery envelope; their foundations sit below ground.
- Nearby broadleaf trees use a reduced CC0 scan of real trunk and branch geometry, with crossed leaf clusters baked from the original tree’s connected leaf geometry and photograph, distributed through the source canopy. The 6–13 m fitted broadleaf population no longer stretches the small source tree into 20 m columns. Near/far asset URLs include their full SHA-256 to replace cached older canopies. Two shared three-draw tiers replace eight nearby trees on mobile / twelve on desktop, plus up to 64 / 128 middle-distance trees. Fuji’s separate pink cutout population is removed. An irregular forest margin grows on the rendered slopes rather than on flattened individual pads. Every selected tree compacts all parts of its original representation out of the fallback draw count; load failures and distant trees retain the fallback. This is a generic deciduous-tree asset, not a claim of botanically exact local species.
- Roadside understorey uses tapered, curved grass blades rooted in the actual verge/shore triangles. A single draw selects at most 384 mobile / 720 desktop tufts near the camera target. Full road, sidewalk and occupied-site clearances are checked; desert and breakwater areas remain unplanted.
- Urban roadside structures now form connected three-unit street blocks with party walls, shared parcels, road-facing windows, projecting floor ledges, colonnades and roof rims. Block-level clearance checks 315 samples over the entire forecourt against the whole road, retaining at least 2.5 m beyond the driving surface. The blocks continue beside elevated roads and rise at least ten metres above the adjacent driving deck; the former random near-road tower boxes are removed. Fuji and Singapore terrace foliage now uses photographed sprays instead of opaque ornamental lumps. Singapore’s unsupported tall bridge portals are removed; its road roughness floor rises from .55 to .74 while retaining the actual photograph and roughness map.
- Nearby urban windows now trace a simple room behind the existing panes, producing view-dependent wall, ceiling and floor shading, rear-wall silhouettes and occasional partial blinds. Instanced façades and merged glazing share the effect. Existing glass reflections and physical lighting remain; room depth is shader-based, with no added geometry, draws or textures. The effect fades from 45 to 100 m, and distant fragments skip the room intersection. Small panes retain a consistent room scale through the existing UV buffer instead of stretching an interior to each aperture.
- Farther broadleaf crowns use alpha-tested photographed leaf sprays on curved geometry. Mobile retains 340 triangles per crown; desktop uses 1,600 instead of the former 2,160. Branches share existing trunk batches. Canopy colour and rotation vary within the existing clearance radius. Summit, Cedar Ridge and Norway now use source-derived CC0 pine trunks and photographed needle clusters at near and middle distances. Four two-batch GLBs preserve their natural proportions through uniform fitting; every original trunk/bough is removed from the fallback batch when selected. Distant or failed-load pines retain the fitted bough fallback. Full fitted bounds are refreshed before culling to prevent premature edge disappearance.
- Coastal water and the Norwegian lake use a shared physically lit water material with a 1.333 index of refraction. Reflection follows the existing venue environment and lighting; crossed irregular ripples replace fixed sunset colouring. Animation uses the existing pause/reduced-motion clock. No reflection render pass or new texture is allocated; nearby objects are not dynamically mirrored.
- Summit’s large repeated rounded rocks now use asymmetric layered outcrops with gullies, broken shoulders and buried bases. Original accepted positions, rotations, clearance envelopes and RNG order are unchanged. Existing CC0 cliff colour/normal maps add rock surface detail, with stable per-face 20 m UV projection. The merged group uses one draw and 208 mobile / 312 desktop triangles per rock, down from 324. This remains authored scenery, not a scanned local cliff.
- Launch ramps now have sidewalls and an underside reaching the existing road, within the original ramp footprint. They add 10 triangles per ramp and no material draw.

The nine original flagship terrace landmarks remain: Fuji's teahouse, ridge observatory and forest beacon; San Francisco's clockhouse, stair hall and wind sculptures; Singapore's petal canopy, glazed prisms and lantern grove. Their finished beds, benches, railings and supported access walks retain the previous reserved footprints and distance culling. Decorative outer-rail openings remain aligned to these walks; driving barriers remain continuous.

Leaf entries also record the alpha source and crop. `scripts/prepare-track-surfaces.mjs` and `scripts/foliage-derivative.mjs` reproduce the outputs and reject unexpected source or output bytes. The optional cliff pair has a separate `public/assets/environments/surfaces/fjord-rock-provenance.json` and `FJORD-ROCK-LICENSE.txt`. Its four desktop/mobile derivatives retain the source's natural colour and record source MD5/SHA-256, output hashes, dimensions, permissions and the OpenGL +Y normal convention. `scripts/prepare-fjord-rock.mjs` reproduces them and verifies source MD5 before processing. This is a reusable photographed cliff material, not a scan of the Norwegian game location. The window-interior shader introduces no external imagery.

Tree source geometry (95 MB) is authoring-only. `scripts/download-tree-source.mjs` verifies primary-source MD5 records into a temporary directory; `scripts/prepare-scanned-trees.mjs` performs attribute-weighted simplification and canopy sampling plus a deterministic source-leaf atlas bake. Durable original inputs can be retained outside the repository using `--source`; none is served by the game. It requires local glTF Transform 4.5.1, meshoptimizer 1.3.0 and sharp packages, passed with `--tools`. `public/assets/environments/trees/provenance.json` records the original sources, leaf photograph, reduced meshes and exact output hashes.

Colour maps use sRGB; normal/roughness maps remain linear, following [Three.js colour management](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/color-management.html). Repeated geometry uses shared instancing or static material batches.

## Measured bounds

Eleven maps are shared per world by default. The desktop transfer is **2,724,098 bytes**; mobile is **606,004 bytes**. Estimated uncompressed RGBA residency including mipmaps is **34⅔ MiB desktop / 8⅔ MiB mobile**, including the two close-ground maps (10⅔ / 2⅔ MiB). Norway and Summit opt into two cliff maps at 1024² desktop / 512² mobile, adding **714,926 / 201,500 bytes** and **10⅔ / 2⅔ MiB**. Their per-world thirteen-map surface total is **3,439,024 bytes / 45⅓ MiB desktop** and **807,504 bytes / 11⅓ MiB mobile**. These are surface estimates, not total GPU-memory measurements. No dynamic lights or shadow maps are added.

The separate tree asset transfers **518,532 bytes mobile / 1,286,196 desktop**, with **2,481 / 5,161 triangles per instance** and five embedded images. Its estimated mipmapped RGBA image residency is **5.67 / 22.67 MiB**. Separate middle-distance variants transfer **65,484 / 160,020 bytes**, with **586 / 917 triangles per instance** and **0.42 / 1.67 MiB** estimated texture residency. Combined tree transfer is **584,016 / 1,446,216 bytes**, with about **6.08 / 24.33 MiB** texture residency. The eight/twelve-slot near tree ceiling is 19,848 / 61,932 triangles; middle-distance adds up to 37,504 / 117,376. Actual fallback draw counts shrink as scan tiers replace their instances. No tree casts an additional dynamic shadow. Grass adds at most 9,216 / 17,280 active triangles in one draw, with no textures. These extra scene tiers are separate from the existing destination-module bound.

The loader reserves final image dimensions before its first GPU allocation, checks decoded dimensions, retains CPU sources for context restoration and disposes maps once. Failed leaf loads retain a shaped cutout fallback rather than opaque rectangular cards. Late loads cannot reattach after disposal.

## Norway relief — deployed and verified live

The follow-up reshapes the Norwegian range's camera-facing slopes with gentler foothills, deeper winding gullies, broader buttresses and an irregular recessed foot. It retains the same two indexed grids, 6,400 mobile / 14,336 desktop triangles and one rock-material draw. Sampled road-center clearance remains at least 89.54 m; driving geometry and recovery behavior are unchanged.

Flatter slopes exposed collapsed triangles in the previous vertical texture projection. The corrected mapping uses Z distance and accumulated cross-slope surface distance, keeping 20 m repeats along each row. Venue batching retains these coordinates for both colour and normal maps. This is not a claim of uniform distortion-free mapping in every direction. Independent checks found no collapsed UV triangles, with projected texture-area ratios of 0.262–1.000003 mobile and 0.239–1.000003 desktop, and unchanged position/normal/index buffers after the UV correction.

The relief pass passed 25 focused geometry, scenery, layout, asset and world-detail tests; the later combined crowd/terrain group passed 60 tests. These overlap and are not a summed test count. After permitted browser access recovered on 5 October, the actual desktop starting-grid and mobile bridge-approach views each loaded all 13 maps with no reported fixture or WebGL errors, and the reviewer checked visible terrain and road clearance. Actual gameplay and native input were also checked at the four required desktop/phone viewport sizes. The updated 34,162-byte Norway preview comes from the settled actual renderer, with 13 positive GPU map probes, ready sky/pines and all 18 crowd assets loaded. The [5 October release report](../reports/crowd-fjord-release-2026-10-05.md) records this evidence, the successful 1,140-test/build/deployment gate, all 106 matching live-byte requests and the production start/pause smoke check.
