# Catalogue environments and textured spectator meshes

Updated 5 October 2026. These are original arcade environments with licensed CC0 MakeHuman human assets. They do not reproduce another game's art or surveyed circuit architecture.

The crowd timing and wardrobe-continuity update has passed renderer and responsive gameplay review. Source commit `a556613` is pushed and deployed after the normal complete test/build gate passed. Exact live-byte verification and the production lobby/start/pause smoke check passed. See the [5 October release report](../reports/crowd-fjord-release-2026-10-05.md) for the evidence and limitations.

## Coverage

All **38 current circuits** now receive coordinated foreground lighting, sky tint, fog, reflection strength and backdrop exposure. Eight regional treatments distinguish maritime, Mediterranean, woodland, alpine, tropical, arid, metropolitan and industrial venues. Harbor, Fuji and San Francisco retain their individually approved grades.

Each route has **three safely placed sector terraces**, for 114 authored sector locations in the catalogue. The architecture follows the region: sail terminals, timber pavilions, motorsport trusses, curved fabric canopies, shaded colonnades, glass viewing galleries or dock signal houses. Sector signs and road-surface wear accompany the buildings. Each terrace has a foundation reaching terrain or below the coastal waterline. Layout checks cover the full route, not only the road's closest nominal segment, and reserve space against grandstands, older landmarks, trees, buildings and trackside services.

This is catalogue-wide regional detail. It is not a claim that all 38 environments contain unique building assets or that they match Asphalt's layouts. Related regions intentionally share architecture and material sets.

The environment pass also adds continuous SF shoreline/inland relief, sidewalk-connected city plots, dedicated CC0 paving and photographed leaf cutouts. See [track visual quality](track-visual-quality.md) for current geometry, texture budgets, source permissions and clearance evidence.

## Nearby spectators

Close spectators use **six individually fitted, textured MakeHuman adults**: three men and three women, with young, middle-aged and older faces, different body builds, hairstyles and everyday clothes. The latest pass replaces two repeated blue/logo tops with an olive jacket and a wine blouse/skirt, and refits the white-tee and striped-shirt adults with different ages and body weights. The CC0 source meshes include real hands, shoes, garment folds, hair cards, eyes and facial topology. These replace the previous smooth procedural close people; that original mesh remains the immediate loading/failure fallback.

Each optimized GLB contains **11,742–14,099 triangles, 53 rig joints and three material draws**. Opaque 1024-pixel clothing/face atlases prevent the blended-face holes seen in the first prototype; 512-pixel depth-writing hair cutouts preserve hair silhouettes without rear-card sorting over faces. The six assets total **8,796,628 bytes**, including the new garment masks. They are requested asynchronously, at most two at once, and geometry/textures are shared by a fixed pool. The race never waits for a crowd download.

Standing poses now preserve each imported inseam and calibrate the shoe sole from the actual foot-weighted geometry. This corrects the short sportswear rig's previous 10 cm floating gap and the taller rigs' unintended crouch. Seated pelvises use fitted garment support offsets above the actual chair top, independently of spectator height: 45.5 cm for the main stands and 39 cm for the paddock. Both hands follow the resulting torso height while feet stay on the row floor. The calibration is cached per source and does not run during animation. Textured spectators receive existing environment shadows, while crowd shadow casting remains disabled.

The pool is still **six characters on mobile and ten on desktop**. The near radius is 22 metres on mobile and 25 on desktop: the widest current roads place front-row seats 18.53 metres from the centreline, outside the old 18-metre phone radius. Every current circuit's real grandstand placement is checked for activation from its driving centreline. Each visible character costs three draws, or four when holding a phone. Fixed slots clone the skeleton and masked body material; source geometry, morph attributes, images and unmodified materials remain shared. Runtime posing uses the imported upper/lower bone lengths, preserving arm anatomy while waving, clapping, raising fists and filming. Palms and fingertips now orient toward the actual gesture, including inward clapping palms and an open raised hand. Fingers curl for a phone/fist, heads and necks follow the passing car, and planted feet support small pelvis shifts with an opposing chest lean. Individual celebration bursts include quiet recovery, and blinking closes smoothly. Legs fold into seats, and two transferred facial targets support blinking and restrained cheering. Near slots keep the same wardrobe as their seat. Changes in people do not create meshes or issue network requests every frame.

All three textured tiers now choose the same one of eight deterministic fabric shades through a shared phase-to-colour helper. Near slots keep an owned colour uniform that updates on reassignment, including assignment before the asset finishes loading. The source-derived mask preserves the existing clothing and selected-footwear choices while protecting exposed skin, eyes and hair. It adds 209,680 raw mask bytes, or 210,736 bytes including GLB container growth, across the six near assets. Original geometry, morph targets and image bytes are preserved. Forty-seven near skin selections are excluded; the wine blouse also has 12 middle and 7 far mask scalars corrected. Six mobile/ten desktop owned body-material clones add no draws or textures and are disposed by their slots; the library still owns the shared source resources.

A **textured middle-distance tier** covers up to **48 additional mobile spectators or 108 on desktop**, within 48/68 metres respectively. All six wardrobes have a reduced human mesh of at most 2,450 triangles; their faces, clothing, hair and shoes reuse the original near-person textures. An offline garment mask supports eight restrained per-seat fabric tints while preserving skin, eyes and hair. These six geometry files total 1,212,612 bytes and contain no images. Eighteen instanced material batches cover all six wardrobes, with no skeleton, Object3D or texture allocated for each spectator.

Beyond that capacity or distance, a **textured far tier** replaces the remaining normal primitive spectators. It uses the same six fitted humans reduced to **769–770 triangles each**, sharing all wardrobe textures and motion palettes with the middle tier. The six far geometry files total **517,912 bytes**, with no duplicate images. Up to **112 far spectators on mobile or 240 on desktop** render within 76/130 metres. Overflow seats are culled instead of reverting to separate head and limb shapes. Selection hysteresis reduces changes near capacity and distance boundaries. The detailed, middle and far tiers are mutually exclusive; drawn-person and capacity-suppressed counts are reported separately. Each textured tier has at most eighteen material batches; foreground counts remain six/ten. Mobile geometry is bounded by 301,400 declared triangles across the three textured tiers, excluding marshals and tiny phone props; the normal assets stay below that bound.

Eight gestures now include relaxed watching, folded hands and small conversation movements as well as waving, clapping, raised fists, filming and both hands raised. Quiet poses are the majority of the deterministic wardrobe profiles. Relaxed standing wrists hang below the waist instead of leaving everyone with hands at their hips. Skirt wearers use a narrower seated knee position. Sixteen seated/standing clips per wardrobe use **24-key, offline-baked half-float motion palettes**, totalling **2,930,688 bytes**. Each person has an independent phase and tempo. Affine matrices use three RGBA texels per bone, reducing matrix fetches by 25% compared with the previous four-texel encoding. Middle and far meshes share the same palette allocation. They interpolate poses on the GPU without individual runtime skeletons.

Middle/far waving, clapping, raised-fist and both-hands gestures now wait at the existing clip's closed seam for a nearby moving car to cross the person's deterministic reaction threshold. Each starts the complete original 5.4 / tempo-second clip, including recovery after the car leaves. Filming, watching, folded hands and conversation retain independent timing. Middle and far share each person's clock, and pause/reduced motion freeze it. This changes clip timing without extra vertex sampling, new instance attributes, palettes or draws. Near/middle poses are still selected by separate systems, so their transition is not guaranteed to be seamless.

The palettes now include the grounded poses. A fourth scalar in the existing instance frame attribute adjusts chair height across body scales while leaving shoe contact fixed; the shader adjusts normals for the small lower-leg deformation. The fixed mobile buffers grow by **11,520 bytes**, with no additional texture, geometry or draw call. Near, middle, far and palette requests share the `2026-10-04-garment-mask-v1` cache version so a returning browser cannot combine incompatible releases. The far mesh shares the middle palette and permits up to 2.5 cm of sole-edge approximation after decimation; the measured worst tested edge is 1.8 cm below the floor, rather than floating above it.

Marshals retain the original orange safety-uniform representation. Primitive spectator batches remain only as immediate loading/error fallback, and submit zero normal spectators once the far assets are available. Pausing, reduced motion, load failure, download deadlines, aborted teardown and late responses are handled explicitly; geometries, textures, skeleton textures, shared motion palettes and phone materials are disposed. Both distance tiers share the library's two-decode limit; variants and motion downloads prepare sequentially after near assets have been queued. A failed asset retains the working fallback.

This is a substantial realistic-anatomy/material upgrade from primitive crowds, **not photorealistic scanned people or film-quality facial animation**. All three normal distance tiers use coherent textured human geometry. Distance people share sixteen motion clips, and near slots use a fixed wardrobe pool rather than a unique human mesh per seat. Capacity limits can thin exceptionally dense stands. Rendering counts do not establish real-phone frame rate, heat or memory behavior, or commercial-game visual parity.

Sources, individual file hashes and authoring details are in `public/assets/crowd/SOURCES.json`; the CC0 license is in `LICENSE-CC0.txt`. Reproduce the near humans with `scripts/prepare-spectator-assets.py`, Blender 4.5 and MPFB 2.0.17 plus the official CC0 system asset pack. The source-atlas garment mask must be present before deriving distance geometry with `scripts/prepare-medium-spectators.py`; that preparation requires and carries the near mask through decimation. The current `scripts/prepare-near-spectator-masks.py` is a pinned upgrade tool: it requires the original unmasked near snapshot, its `SOURCES.json`, and original wine middle/far files, using `scripts/spectator-skin-exclusions.json` to apply verified exclusions. It is not a generic mask generator for arbitrary newly exported meshes. Run `node scripts/bake-crowd-motion.mjs` when rebuilding motion palettes and their source manifest. The mask-only update preserved existing geometry, morphs, images and motion palettes; provenance records the original binary prefixes and the exact 19 distance-mask corrections. Only exported CC0 assets are shipped; MPFB authoring code is not included in the game.
